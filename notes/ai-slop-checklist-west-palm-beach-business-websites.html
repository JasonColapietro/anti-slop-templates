Avoiding AI slop in web design: a checklist for West Palm Beach business websites

The checks Suede AI runs before a Palm Beach County business site goes live, so it reads as a real company to customers and to AI answer engines.

By Jason Colapietro, Founder and CEO, Suede AI · November 14, 2026 · West Palm Beach, Florida

Picture a homeowner in West Palm Beach comparing three local firms on a phone, parked outside a coffee shop on Clematis Street. Two of the sites open on the same purple gradient, the same centered headline floating over a blurred blob and the same three feature cards with emoji icons. The third looks like someone made decisions, and that is the one that gets the call.

This checklist is how Suede AI keeps client sites in the third group. It comes from the same rules Jason Colapietro used to build the Anti-Slop Templates in this repository, and every item can be checked by a business owner in an afternoon.

What "AI slop" looks like on a business website

The short definition Jason Colapietro wrote into this repository's README: the generic AI look is not one bad choice. It is the absence of choices: a gradient nobody picked, Inter on white, identical card grids, uniform corner radii and the same soft shadow on every element. Generators and page builders default to these patterns because they offend no one. They also say nothing about the business.

The cure is commitment. The two systems in this repo, Editorial and Suede, each pick one specific direction and carry it across four surfaces: a landing page, an article or docs page, a pricing page and a dashboard. A local business site needs the same discipline at a smaller scale.

It matters more here than it seems. Palm Beach County buyers often look a company up on Google, ask an AI assistant about it, then open the site. Each step is a credibility test. Generic design tells a reader nobody was paying attention. Generic copy tells an AI answer engine there is nothing specific to cite.

The visual checklist

  1. One accent color, used with meaning. Editorial uses a single persimmon; the Suede system reserves cyan for live registry data. If your site uses five accent colors, none of them mean anything.
  2. A type pairing with character. Fraunces with Hanken Grotesk, or Instrument Serif with Manrope and a mono face. Default system sans on white is the most common tell of an untouched template.
  3. Asymmetry somewhere above the fold. A centered headline over a blob is the template default. Offset the headline, let an image break the grid, or lead with a real number.
  4. Radii and shadows that vary by role. Buttons, cards and panels should not all share one corner radius and one drop shadow. Hierarchy comes from difference.
  5. Real photography or none. A stock handshake undercuts a local firm. A photo of your actual storefront in Northwood Village or your crew on a job in Jupiter does more for trust than any illustration.
  6. No emoji bullets. Use numerals, hairline rules or plain sentences. Emoji lists are one of the fastest ways a page reads as generated.

The content checklist

  1. A first sentence that names who you serve and where. "Commercial cleaning for offices in West Palm Beach and Palm Beach Gardens" beats "Excellence in every detail." AI answer engines quote the specific version.
  2. Claims with sources. Every number or recognition should link to where it can be checked. Suede AI holds itself to this: when The Manifest ranked Suede AI SEO #1 among Palm Beach County-based companies listed in its Florida AI Deployment directory on October 31, 2026, and top 25 statewide, the company linked the directory itself and a recognition page instead of a vague badge.   3. One canonical page per person. If an owner or founder appears on the site, give them one page and point every other mention at it. Jason Colapietro does this with his founder page at Suede AI, so details never drift between sites.
  4. No invented reviews, logos or awards. The FTC's rule on fake reviews and testimonials applies to small businesses too, and AI systems increasingly cross-check claims against other sources. Leave it out if you cannot prove it.

The technical checklist

  1. An accessibility floor. Semantic landmarks, a real heading order, visible focus rings, a skip link and full support for reduced-motion settings. Every page in these templates ships with all five, and none of them change how the site looks to a sighted mouse user.
  2. Speed without a framework where you can. The templates share about 3KB of vanilla JavaScript and animate only transform and opacity. A brochure site for a Jupiter boutique rarely needs more.
  3. Structured data that points to real records. Describe the business and its owner with schema that matches your Google Business Profile, list only the towns you actually serve in areaServed, and never publish an address you cannot staff.

How to run it in one afternoon

Open your homepage on a phone and take a screenshot. Mark each of the thirteen items pass or fail. Fix copy first, because it is the cheapest change and the one AI answer engines notice most. Then fix color and type, which in a token-based system means editing one file: in these templates, a single styles/tokens.css reskins all four surfaces. Leave structural layout changes for last.
